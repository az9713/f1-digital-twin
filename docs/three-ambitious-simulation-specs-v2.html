Formula One Digital Twin Pro

From a playable single car on a track to a full living race weekend: a transient multi-body car, CFD-derived aerodynamics, a rubbering-in track with weather, and a grid of AI opponents fighting in each other's dirty air.

Kept from v1 (foundation, unchanged): browser-first Three.js/TypeScript stack, GLTF car with force-arrow / heat-map / telemetry overlays, OpenF1 ghost-lap replay, Pacejka-based tire core, three-node thermal starting point, strategy sandbox concept, pause/scrub/inspect at any moment. v1's Phases 0–6 become v2's Milestone A.
Non-negotiable constraint (strengthened): The car remains the embodiment of the equations — but now every upgraded model must also upgrade what you see. Combined-slip tires show a live friction ellipse on each contact patch; the CFD-derived aero map renders as streamlines and a floor-pressure carpet; dirty air is a visible wake volume; track rubbering-in is a darkening racing line. No model ships without its visualization.

1. Detailed Description

v2 turns the digital twin of a car into a digital twin of a race. The player still drives a single machine whose every force is computed and rendered — but that machine now lives on an evolving track, in traffic, across a structured weekend, inside a season. The transient tire model makes warm-up and degradation a skill; the aero surrogate makes following another car a physical penalty and a tow a physical gift; the multi-body chassis makes the setup sheet a real instrument. The educational thesis is unchanged and amplified: nothing is scripted that can be simulated, and nothing is simulated that cannot be seen.

Two play identities are supported from the same core: Driver (drive every lap, feel every model through the controls and force feedback) and Race Engineer (delegate driving to the AI at a fitted skill level and play the strategy, setup, and telemetry game — the v1 Strategy Sandbox grown into a full profession).

2. Core Physics (Research-grade, still real-time)

  • Tire model: Magic Formula 6.x with combined slip via the friction ellipse, load- and camber-dependent coefficients, first-order relaxation-length transients per tire. Ring-discretized tread thermal network (8×3 nodes) with per-node sliding-power heat input; grip and wear computed from local node temperature. Abrasive + thermal wear with an explicit modeled cliff. Flat-spot geometry from lock-ups feeds a vibration signal to camera and haptics.
  • Aerodynamics: surrogate model trained on an offline OpenFOAM campaign over a public open-wheel geometry, inputs (hf, hr, roll, yaw, steer, DRS) → outputs (CL,f, CL,r, CD, aero balance). Floor-stall hysteresis produces porpoising when spring/ride-height choices permit it. Wake model: downstream downforce deficit and drag reduction as fitted functions of longitudinal gap and lateral offset.
  • Chassis: 6-DOF rigid chassis + 4 corner assemblies with double-wishbone kinematic tables (camber/toe/scrub vs. travel), nonlinear dampers (bump/rebound, low/high speed), anti-roll bars, and kerb excitation sampled from the track mesh at 1 kHz on a physics worker.
  • Driveline & power unit: engine torque map with ERS-K/MGU-H deployment modes, gearshift torque interruption, limited-slip differential (preload / power / coast ramps), brake-by-wire rear axle with migration. Battery SOC, temperature, and per-lap deployment limits as in v1, now with a deployment-strategy optimizer the Race Engineer can override.
  • Track & weather: grip field over the track mesh updated each lap (rubber laid ∝ sliding energy of all cars), marbles offline, water-depth field driven by a scenario rain timeline with drying ∝ traffic + temperature; tire model reads local grip and water depth under each contact patch.
  • AI drivers: two-stage — offline minimum-curvature line + quasi-steady-state lap-time optimization per car state; online path-following controller with tire-state-aware braking points, defending/overtaking decision layer consuming the wake model.

The player still sequences burns and watches geometry — but the geometry is now flown on real ephemerides, targeted with Lambert arcs picked off an interactive porkchop surface, and navigated under visible uncertainty. The campaign teaches the real historical staircase of capability, and the endgame is the full MSR architecture: land on real terrain, operate on the surface, fly the MAV to a rendezvous the player must close with Clohessy–Wiltshire proximity dynamics, and bring the canister home through a guided Earth entry.

The educational thesis widens from "the tyranny of the rocket equation" to "the tyranny of coupled budgets": mass buys margin, margin costs money, money costs schedule, schedule crosses launch windows, and every choice is visible as geometry, color, and a Pareto point on the program scoreboard.
