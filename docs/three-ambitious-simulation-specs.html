Formula One Digital Twin

A living, playable F1 car whose every force, heat, and tire slip is computed from first principles and rendered as a visible, responsive machine.

Non-negotiable constraint: The user must see a concrete race car (3D GLTF model) moving on a track. Physics is overlaid as force arrows, heat maps, tire contact patches, and real-time telemetry. Numbers alone are not enough — the car is the embodiment of the equations.

1. Detailed Description

This project builds a real-time, browser-based digital twin of a modern Formula One car. The twin is driven by simplified but physically grounded models of tire mechanics, aerodynamics, thermal dynamics, and vehicle dynamics. The experience is deliberately game-like: the player can drive, choose tire compounds, set brake bias, and invent pit strategies, then immediately see the consequences on lap time, tire degradation, and energy deployment.

The educational goal is to make the invisible forces of high-speed racing visible and intuitive. Why does a soft tire go faster for three laps and then fall off a cliff? Why does a higher ride height kill top speed? Why does energy recovery under braking feel different in wet conditions? Every answer is shown as a moving arrow, a glowing contact patch, or a changing color on the car body.

2. Core Physics (Rigorous but Real-Time Capable)

  • Tire model: Simplified Pacejka “Magic Formula” for longitudinal and lateral force as a function of slip ratio / slip angle, vertical load, and temperature. A brush-model contact patch is visualized.
  • Thermal model: Three-node thermal network (surface / bulk / carcass) with heat generation from sliding friction and cooling from convection + radiation. Temperature feeds back into grip and wear rate.
  • Aero: Ride-height and rake dependent drag and downforce coefficients. Front / rear balance is adjustable. DRS is a discrete state change.
  • Vehicle dynamics: Bicycle model + load transfer (longitudinal and lateral). Suspension is spring-damper with simple anti-roll.
  • Energy: ERS-K recovery under braking, MGU-H simplified as a power source, battery SOC and thermal limits.
Fx = Dx sin(Cx arctan(Bx κ − Ex(Bx κ − arctan(Bx κ))))   (longitudinal Pacejka)

3. Visualization & Game-like Interaction

The car is a real 3D asset (GLTF). The camera can be free, chase, or onboard. Force vectors (aero, tire forces, load transfer) are drawn as colored arrows. Tire temperature is shown as a heat-map on the contact patch and sidewall. A telemetry strip shows speed, throttle, brake, gear, tire temp, and SOC in real time. The player can pause, scrub time, and inspect any force at any moment.

Game modes: free practice, qualifying, race against a historical lap (OpenF1 data), and “Strategy Sandbox” where the player designs a full race strategy and watches the car execute it under variable weather and fuel load.

4. Implementation Plan

Phase 0 — Foundations (1 week)Project scaffold (Vite + Three.js + TypeScript). Load a free GLTF F1-style car. Basic track (simple oval or Spa sector). Camera controls.
Phase 1 — Telemetry Ingestion (1 week)OpenF1 client. Pull historical sessions, car data, position, weather. Store as time-series. Replay a real lap as a “ghost”.
Phase 2 — Vehicle Dynamics Core (2 weeks)Bicycle model + load transfer. Integrate simple Pacejka. Visualize force arrows. Match a straight-line acceleration and a constant-radius corner to OpenF1 data.
Phase 3 — Thermal + Wear (1.5 weeks)Three-node tire thermal model. Wear rate proportional to sliding power and temperature. Surface heat-map on the 3D tire geometry.
Phase 4 — Aero + ERS (1.5 weeks)Ride-height / rake aero maps. DRS. Simplified ERS recovery and battery SOC. Power limits and thermal derating.
Phase 5 — Strategy Layer + Game Modes (2 weeks)Pit-stop model, compound selection, fuel. Strategy Sandbox UI. “Beat the historical lap” challenge with scoring.
Phase 6 — Polish, Docs, Validation Report (1 week)Performance profiling (target 30–60 Hz on mid-range laptop). Validation notebook comparing simulated vs OpenF1 telemetry. Full README + physics notes.

5. Deliverables

  • Single-page web application (browser) with interactive 3D car and force overlays
  • OpenF1 data pipeline and offline session cache
  • Physics engine module (TypeScript) with unit tests against known cornering and braking cases
  • Validation report: simulated vs real lap times and tire temperature traces
  • Educational “Physics Notes” page explaining every model with diagrams
  • Strategy Sandbox mode that lets a user design and evaluate a full race strategy

Mars Sample Return Mission Planner

Design, fly, and recover a complete sample-return campaign from Earth to Mars and back — every burn, every mass trade, every failure mode made visible and playable.

Non-negotiable constraint: The user must see concrete spacecraft (landers, ascent vehicles, orbiters, Earth-return capsules) moving through space. Trajectories, burns, and gravitational spheres of influence are rendered as living geometry, not abstract numbers. Physics is the game.

1. Detailed Description

This project is an interactive, educational mission-design environment for a full Mars Sample Return (MSR) architecture. The player designs the mass budget, chooses propulsion systems, sequences the burns, and then watches the campaign unfold in a 3-D solar-system view. Every delta-v, every gravity loss, every thermal constraint is computed from classical orbital mechanics and rendered as geometry and color.

The educational goal is to make the tyranny of the rocket equation, the geometry of Hohmann and ballistic transfers, and the difficulty of planetary entry, descent and landing feel visceral. Success is measured by sample mass returned, mission duration, and robustness to injected faults (engine under-performance, navigation error, dust-storm delay).

2. Core Physics

  • Orbital mechanics: Two-body Keplerian propagation + patched-conic approximation for planetary flybys and captures. Optional n-body validation against SPICE kernels.
  • Propulsion: Tsiolkovsky rocket equation with realistic Isp and structural mass fractions. Finite-burn gravity losses for low-thrust phases.
  • Entry, Descent, Landing: Simplified ballistic entry + parachute + powered descent. Heating rate and g-load constraints.
  • Ascent: Mars ascent vehicle with gravity turn and staging. Atmosphere model (exponential).
  • Navigation & faults: Simple error models (Δv error, timing error) that the player must design margins against.
Δv = Isp · g0 · ln(m0 / mf)   +   gravity & drag losses

3. Visualization & Game-like Interaction

The solar system is rendered with correct relative scales for orbits (planets are enlarged for visibility). Spacecraft are concrete 3-D models. Burns appear as thrust plumes and instantaneous Δv vectors. Spheres of influence are translucent shells. The player can scrub time, freeze, and inspect any orbital element or mass state. A “Mission Timeline” view shows the sequence of events with mass remaining and Δv budget.

Game modes: free design sandbox, “NASA Reference MSR” challenge (match or beat published Δv and mass), and “Fault Injection” mode where random under-performance events force real-time re-planning.

Figure 2 — Patched-conic Mars Sample Return geometry. Outbound Hohmann transfer, capture, surface operations (not shown), ascent, and Earth-return trajectory.

4. Implementation Plan

Phase 0 — Foundations (1 week)Three.js solar-system view with correct orbital periods (accelerated time). Simple spacecraft GLTF. Camera + time controls.
Phase 1 — Two-Body + Patched Conics (2 weeks)Keplerian propagator. Sphere-of-influence transitions. Hohmann and bi-elliptic transfer calculators. Visual Δv vectors.
Phase 2 — Propulsion & Mass Budget (1.5 weeks)Rocket equation UI. Stage mass fractions. Finite-burn approximation for long low-thrust arcs. Live mass remaining display.
Phase 3 — Planetary Arrival & EDL (2 weeks)Entry corridor, heating rate, parachute + powered descent. Surface stay timer. Ascent vehicle simulation with gravity turn.
Phase 4 — Return & Earth Capture (1.5 weeks)Mars departure window, Earth return trajectory, direct entry or aerocapture options. Sample canister mass tracking.
Phase 5 — Fault Injection & Scoring (1.5 weeks)Random Δv errors, timing slips, dust-storm delays. Player must replan. Scoring on sample mass, duration, and robustness.
Phase 6 — SPICE Validation + Docs (1 week)Compare selected trajectories against NASA SPICE kernels / GMAT. Educational notes on every model. README + validation notebook.

5. Deliverables

  • Interactive 3-D solar-system mission planner (browser or Electron)
  • Full mass-budget and Δv calculator with live visualization
  • Fault-injection game mode with scoring
  • Validation report against open SPICE / GMAT cases
  • Educational “Orbital Mechanics Notes” with diagrams of every transfer type used
  • Reference MSR architecture that the player can load and improve upon

eVTOL Aircraft Design Studio

Design your own electric vertical-takeoff aircraft, fly it through cities, hear its noise footprint, and feel every trade-off between range, battery heat, and community acceptance.

Non-negotiable constraint: The aircraft must be a concrete, articulated 3-D model whose rotors spin, whose battery temperature is visible as a heat map, and whose acoustic footprint is drawn on the ground as a living contour. Physics is embodied in the flying machine the player can see and hear.

1. Detailed Description

This project is a physics-based eVTOL design and flight studio. The player chooses configuration (multirotor, lift+cruise, tilt-rotor), sizes batteries and motors, sets rotor parameters, and then flies realistic missions over city maps. The simulation computes thrust, power, battery thermal state, and far-field noise in real time. The educational goal is to make the central tension of urban air mobility tangible: more power gives better hover performance but more noise and faster battery heat; larger batteries give range but kill payload and increase disk loading.

Every design decision is immediately visible as a change in the flight envelope, the acoustic contour on the ground, and the color of the battery pack. The experience is game-like: design challenges, “quietest flight over a school zone,” range-maximization under a noise constraint, and emergency landing under partial power loss.

2. Core Physics

  • Aerodynamics / Propulsion: Simplified Blade Element Momentum Theory (BEMT) for each rotor. Induced power, profile power, and climb power. Transition models for tilt and lift+cruise configurations.
  • Battery: Equivalent-circuit (RC) model with open-circuit voltage, internal resistance, and thermal capacity. Heat generation = I²R + irreversible losses. Cooling via convection.
  • Acoustics: Semi-empirical rotor noise model (loading + thickness noise). Ground footprint computed by spherical spreading + simple atmospheric absorption. Validated against NASA STRIVES / Joby open acoustic data.
  • Flight dynamics: 6-DOF rigid body with simplified actuator dynamics. Hover, transition, and cruise modes.
Pinduced ≈ T · √(T / (2 ρ A))    +   profile & climb terms from BEMT

3. Visualization & Game-like Interaction

The aircraft is a fully articulated 3-D model. Rotors spin at the correct RPM. Battery temperature is shown as a color gradient on the pack. Acoustic footprint is a real-time contour map projected onto the city terrain (color = dBA). The player can fly free, follow mission waypoints, or run automated design-of-experiments to explore the Pareto front of range vs noise vs payload.

Game modes: “Design Challenge” (meet a range and noise target), “City Mission” (fly a realistic vertiport-to-vertiport route while staying under a community noise limit), and “Failure Mode” (lose one rotor or one battery string and still land safely).

Figure 3 — eVTOL in hover with thrust vectors, battery thermal state, and ground acoustic footprint. Flight-phase labels show the full mission profile the player must master.

4. Implementation Plan

Phase 0 — Foundations (1 week)Three.js scene with city terrain (simple heightmap or OSM-derived). Load articulated eVTOL GLTF. Basic 6-DOF free flight.
Phase 1 — Rotor & Power Model (2 weeks)BEMT implementation for multi-rotor. Power vs thrust curves. Visual RPM and thrust arrows. Match published hover power data.
Phase 2 — Battery Thermal (1.5 weeks)RC battery model + thermal network. Heat-map on the 3-D battery pack. Derating under high temperature.
Phase 3 — Acoustics (2 weeks)Semi-empirical noise model. Real-time ground contour. Validation against NASA open acoustic datasets (STRIVES / Joby).
Phase 4 — Transition & Cruise (1.5 weeks)Tilt or lift+cruise mode switching. Aero drag in forward flight. Full mission energy integration.
Phase 5 — Design Studio + Challenges (2 weeks)Parameter UI for rotor size, battery capacity, configuration. Automated Pareto exploration. City mission scoring (noise + range + safety).
Phase 6 — Polish, Docs, Validation (1 week)Performance target 30 Hz. Validation notebook vs NASA data. Educational notes on BEMT, battery heat, and community noise metrics.

5. Deliverables

  • Interactive eVTOL design and flight studio (browser or desktop)
  • Articulated 3-D aircraft with live thermal and acoustic overlays
  • Design-of-experiments tool that explores the range–noise–payload Pareto front
  • Validation report against open NASA eVTOL acoustic and performance data
  • Educational “eVTOL Physics Notes” covering BEMT, battery thermal dynamics, and noise metrics
  • Three ready-to-play challenge scenarios (quiet school-zone transit, maximum range, one-rotor-out emergency)

All three projects are designed to be solo-feasible, runnable on a mid-range laptop (RTX 3090 class), and validated exclusively against freely available open data.
