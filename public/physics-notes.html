Physics Notes

Every model in the F1 Digital Twin, the equation it solves, and where to see it on the car. Nothing on screen is faked: arrows, colors, and timers are read out of the same state the simulation integrates at 120 Hz.

1 · Vehicle dynamics — the single-track model

The chassis is a rigid body with three planar degrees of freedom: forward speed vx, lateral speed vy, and yaw rate r. The four wheels are lumped into one front and one rear axle (a "bicycle"). Newton–Euler in the body frame:

m(v̇x − vyr) = FxR + FxFcos δ − FyFsin δ − Fdrag − Froll
m(v̇y + vxr) = FyFcos δ + FyR + FxFsin δ
Izz ṙ = a(FyFcos δ + FxFsin δ) − b FyR

δ is the steering angle at the front wheels, a and b the distances from the center of gravity to each axle. Braking shifts vertical load forward (m·ax·h/L), which is why the rear goes light — and loose — under heavy braking.

See it: press F — purple arrows are axle lateral forces, green is drive/brake, orange is drag, cyan is downforce pressing the car down.

2 · Tires — the Pacejka "Magic Formula"

Each axle's lateral force comes from the slip angle α — the difference between where the tire points and where it travels:

αF = atan((vy + a·r)/vx) − δ     αR = atan((vy − b·r)/vx)
Fy = −D sin(C atan(Bα − E(Bα − atan Bα))),   D = μFz

Force rises almost linearly with slip, peaks near 6–8°, then falls — that falling side is a slide. Longitudinal and lateral demands share one budget (the friction circle): using the tires to brake steals exactly the grip you wanted for turning:

(Fx/μFz)² + (Fy/μFz)² ≤ 1
See it: trail-brake into Turn 1 — the purple front arrow shrinks while you brake hard, and grows as you release. That is the friction circle trading braking for turning.

3 · Tire temperature — a three-node thermal network

Each axle's tire is three coupled heat stores: a thin surface (tread face), the rubber bulk, and the carcass. Sliding friction pumps heat into the surface; airflow removes it at a rate proportional to speed:

CsṪs = Pslide − hs(v)(Ts−Tair) − ksb(Ts−Tb) − ktrack(Ts−Ttrack)
CbṪb = ksb(Ts−Tb) − kbc(Tb−Tc)    CcṪc = kbc(Tb−Tc) − hc(v)(Tc−Tair)

Grip depends on bulk temperature through a Gaussian window around each compound's optimum (soft ≈ 95 °C, hard ≈ 105 °C). Cold tires keep only ~72 % of peak grip — the out-lap is slow for a physical reason. Wear accumulates with sliding energy, faster when the surface overheats; past 65 % wear the grip falls off a cliff.

See it: wheel color and the bottom-left widget (blue→green→yellow→red). Do three hard laps on softs and watch the wear bar amber, then the lap times fall away.

4 · Aerodynamics — ride height, rake, DRS

Downforce and drag scale with dynamic pressure ½ρv², through areas CLA and CDA that depend on ride height: run the floor lower and it works harder (~1.6 % load per mm), until below ~25 mm it stalls. Rake (rear height − front) shifts the aero balance forward. DRS opens the rear wing: ~12 % less drag, ~0.9 m² of CLA dumped.

Fdown = ½ρ CLA(hF,hR,DRS) v²     Fdrag = ½ρ CDA(h,DRS) v²

At 335 km/h the model's downforce is ≈ 2.6× the car's weight — this is why braking from top speed can exceed 5 g while a road car manages 1.

See it: the cyan downforce arrow grows with the square of speed. Open DRS (E) on the straight: top speed rises ~15 km/h; brake or steer and it snaps shut.

5 · ERS — the energy budget

The MGU-K moves energy between the rear axle and a 4 MJ battery at up to 120 kW, both ways. Braking harvests (≤ 2 MJ/lap into the store), full throttle deploys (≤ 4 MJ/lap out). Modes: balanced guards a 30 % reserve, hotlap spends everything, harvest only collects.

SOĊ = Pharvest − Pdeploy,   |P| ≤ 120 kW,   Edeploy,lap ≤ 4 MJ
See it: the battery bar top-right of the tire widget — cyan while harvesting under braking, amber while deploying. Cycle modes with Q.

6 · Strategy — racing the tire model

The Strategy Sandbox (T) races two stint plans through the same thermal + wear model you drive on. Each simulated lap advances the tire state with a representative load profile, then prices the lap:

tlap = tbase + kgrip(gref − g(T, wear)) + kfuelmfuel (+ 24 s pit)

Cold out-laps, degradation slopes, the soft-tire cliff, and fuel burn-off all emerge from the model — the crossover lap where a one-stop beats a no-stop is a computed result, not a script.

7 · Validation against reality

The track is fitted from OpenF1 GPS of Verstappen's fastest race lap at Monza 2024; the car parameters are public-ballpark figures, not fitted to the lap. A quasi-steady-state lap simulation with exactly the game's parameters gives:
